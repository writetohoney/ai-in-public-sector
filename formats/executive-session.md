# Executive Session — Can Your Institution Adapt?

### A 90-minute guided self-assessment for senior public-sector leaders

**Audience:** deputy ministers, ADMs, agency heads, CxOs, and their direct reports · **Length:** 90 minutes · **Group:** works from ~8 up to a room of 40 · **Prerequisite:** none

---

> **This is an overlay, not a standalone curriculum.** It reframes the course's [adaptive capacity diagnostic](../resources/adaptive-capacity-diagnostic.md) for senior leaders in a single sitting. The seven dimensions, their questions, and the H2+/H2− test all come from that instrument — this file only specifies how to run them in 90 minutes for people who set the conditions everything else operates under. When the diagnostic changes, this inherits the change; keep the definitions there, not here.

## Why this exists

Senior leaders will not take a fourteen-week seminar or a three-day intensive. But they are the people who sign the procurement, set the risk appetite, and decide whether a failure gets learned from or buried. The other formats teach people to *assess* institutions; this one puts the assessment in front of the people who can actually *change* one — and does it in the only slot they will give you.

The design bet: executives disengage when they are an audience and perform when they are in an open discussion. So they do neither. They assess their own institution, privately, in real time, one dimension at a time. The product of the session is not knowledge transferred — it is the discomfort of the questions they cannot answer, felt in the room, next to a peer who cannot answer them either.

## What a participant leaves with

- A private, seven-dimension read on whether their own institution can adapt — and, more usefully, a specific list of the things they could not answer
- The single question to ask their organization on Monday for each blind spot
- One distinction that reframes every AI proposal that crosses their desk: does this build capacity, or just relieve the pressure that would force real change?

## The 90 minutes

### 0:00 – 0:10 · Frame

The gap this addresses is not a technology gap; it is an adaptation gap. Institutions built for stability, legibility, and accountability are being asked to absorb something fast, opaque, and consequential. AI is the stress test, not the subject — it presses on weaknesses slower technologies let you leave unexamined.

Set the two failure modes to avoid — hype and reflexive fear — and set the rule for the next hour: **you will assess your own institution, privately, and no one will be asked to share a score.** The honesty only works if it stays theirs.

Hand out the scoring card (below). One line per dimension.

### 0:10 – 0:65 · The seven dimensions (≈8 minutes each)

For each dimension, the facilitator runs the same tight loop:

1. **Name it** (1 min) — the dimension and the question it asks, from the [diagnostic](../resources/adaptive-capacity-diagnostic.md).
2. **The sharp question** (2 min) — pose the one question below and let it land. These are chosen to be answerable only with evidence, so the honest response is often "I don't actually know."
3. **Private rating** (2 min) — each participant marks their own institution on the card: *strong / uneven / weak / can't answer*, plus the one piece of evidence or the blank where it should be.
4. **Surfacing** (3 min) — not a score share. The facilitator asks only: *"How many of you wrote 'can't answer'?"* and takes two or three comments on why. The point is the recognition that the blanks are shared and structural.

The seven, with the question that does the work:

| # | Dimension | The question to pose |
|---|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Sensing** | Where did your last three technology decisions originate — inside the organization, or with a vendor who came to you? |
| 2 | **Self-legibility** | Could your organization produce an accurate inventory of its own systems and data holdings? How long would it take, and who would you have to ask? |
| 3 | **Absorptive capacity** | If your largest technology vendor walked away tomorrow, could you evaluate or replace what they do? |
| 4 | **Structural flexibility** | How long from "we should do this" to money and people actually moving? What is the fastest you have ever done it? |
| 5 | **Acting under uncertainty** | When did your organization last run something that was genuinely allowed to fail — and what happened to the people who ran it? |
| 6 | **Feedback & correction** | How does your organization find out it has harmed someone? How long does that take, and does it require a journalist? |
| 7 | **Legitimacy** | Would your current technology decisions survive being reported accurately? What would it take for the public to stop accepting them? |

### 0:65 – 0:80 · The H2+/H2− test

The reframe they take back to the job. Most reform that looks like progress — a rescue team, a special hiring authority, a successful pilot, a better web form — quietly relieves the pressure that would otherwise force structural change, and so extends the life of the system that produced the problem (Pahlka's *Three Horizons*: this is H2−). Genuine capacity-building (H2+) is rarer, slower, and less legible.

Each participant looks at their own weakest dimension and the fix they would instinctively reach for, and asks: *does this build capacity, or relieve pressure?* Most discover their instinct is H2−. That is the session's sharpest moment, and it is the one worth ending near.

### 0:80 – 0:90 · Close — Monday's question

No action plan; executives will not honour one written in a workshop. Instead, each participant leaves with **one question per blind spot** to ask their own organization this week — the "can't answer" lines from their card, turned into the question that would fill them. The facilitator names the through-line: they do not need to become technical to govern this well; they need to keep asking who is accountable, who bears the cost, and whether the fix in front of them changes anything or just buys time.

## Facilitator notes

- **Protect the privacy.** The moment you ask anyone to say their score aloud, the seniority in the room turns it into performance and the honesty evaporates. Only ever ask for a show of hands on "can't answer."
- **Let the silences sit.** The value is in the questions they cannot answer. Do not rescue the room from that discomfort by moving on quickly or supplying reassurance.
- **Keep to eight minutes.** Seven dimensions in 55 minutes only works if you are disciplined; a dimension that runs long steals from the H2 reframe, which is the part they will remember.
- **Guest framing, optional.** A short opening from a leader who has been through an institutional failure — candidly, under the Chatham House Rule — sharpens the room faster than any slide.
- **This is diagnosis, not consulting.** The session deliberately stops at "here are your blind spots and Monday's questions." Offers of follow-up advisory work are a separate conversation, kept out of the room.
- **Definitions live in the [diagnostic](../resources/adaptive-capacity-diagnostic.md).** If a participant wants the full dimension write-up, that is the handout — this session is the compressed, self-applied version.
